# Persona presets

Use these when the content clearly belongs to one of these shows. Otherwise build
personas from the niche using the three-way spread in SKILL.md. Adjust freely —
these are starting points, not fixed casting.

## The Quiet Sage — Jungian psychology / shadow work, English, US/UK

**Dana, 34, Manchester.** Two years into therapy, reads about attachment styles at
night. She's the intended viewer and she's generous — but she's also heard the same
five Jung quotes on every channel in this niche. Leaves the moment it sounds
recycled or self-help-generic. Wants to feel *seen*, not lectured.

**Marcus, 41, Denver.** Playing it while cooking, screen face-down half the time.
Never sees the visuals for long stretches, so a script that leans on the images to
carry meaning loses him. Leaves if the audio has a stretch of ambient filler with
no idea in it.

**Priya, 27, Toronto.** Came from a search like "why do I sabotage relationships".
Wants the actual answer, not a definition of the shadow. Highly allergic to
"but first, let's understand what the unconscious really is". Bounces at the first
sign of padding before the payoff.

## Velvet Anchor — why competence doesn't turn into money

**Ken, 38, Seattle.** Senior engineer, repeatedly passed over. Nodding hard if the
mechanics are specific and real. The instant a case study sounds invented or a
statistic sounds too neat, he checks the description for sources — and if the claim
can't be verified, he distrusts the rest of the episode.

**Sofia, 45, Chicago.** Middle manager, listening on a commute. She's on the other
side of the same dynamic. If the episode reads as pure resentment against
management with no mechanism, she leaves — she wanted to understand a system, not
hear a complaint.

**Tomas, 29, Berlin.** Came from a strong title promising a single case. Twenty
minutes is a big ask. He is counting the seconds to the first concrete detail of
the actual case and will not sit through four minutes of framing.

## Dreamscape Soul — utility / healing ambient (@kasoul-t8n)

Judge by use, not by story. Retention here comes from being left running.

**Nan, 31, Bangkok.** Insomnia, 2am, screen brightness low. Anything with a sudden
transient, a bright visual flash, or a loop seam that snaps her back to alert is
fatal. She needs the first 30 seconds to signal "safe to fall asleep to".

**Alex, 24, remote worker.** Uses it as a focus bed for four-hour blocks. Notices
repetition at the 20-minute mark and any melodic element that pulls attention.
Wants a track that disappears.

**Mei, 48, casual browser.** Found it on Browse, clicked for the thumbnail, will
give it 40 seconds to decide whether this is a channel she returns to. Judges
quality by the visual loop and the first chord, not the title.

## Thai-language content

When the script is Thai, do not translate a Western persona and rename it. Cast
Thai viewers with real context — commute on the BTS, listening between shifts,
watching on a phone with data limits — and let the tone expectations differ.
Formality that reads as authoritative in English can read as stiff or preachy in
Thai, and a hook that works in English often needs to be blunter in Thai.
