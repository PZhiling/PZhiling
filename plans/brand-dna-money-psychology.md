## 1. Show Identity
- Format: Faceless narrative podcast, English, 18–24 min episodes
- Niche: Why competence does not convert into money. The gap between being good at the work and being paid for it — measured in salaries, savings, promotions, and the decisions people avoid making. Behavioural finance and workplace psychology appear only as working tools, never as citations.
- One-line promise: "Every episode explains a money decision the listener has already made, and could not explain at the time."
- Why the show is called Velvet Anchor: three meanings, all load-bearing. *Anchor* is the narrator who holds the episode. *Anchor* is what keeps a capable person in place for years. And *anchoring* is the bias at the centre of the subject — the first number spoken sets every number after it, in a salary, a price, a raise. Use the third meaning as a recurring idea across the season, never as a segment name and never explained twice.
- Host persona: A calm, intelligent storyteller — like a trusted friend explaining something fascinating late at night. Never a lecturer, never a hype-man, never a coach, and never a finance guru. No predictions, no tickers, no portfolio advice.

## 2. Audience
- 25–40, competent and hard-working — often the reliable one on the team, and quietly frustrated that the effort has not turned into recognition, money, or momentum
- Salaried, numerate, financially literate enough to know the standard advice — and still not doing it
- They listen while commuting, doing chores, or winding down at night
- They have already heard "spend less than you earn" and it did not solve anything. They are not here for a budget template; they are here for an explanation of why they did what they did.
- Emotional payoff per episode: first the relief of being understood, then one concrete thing they can do differently on Monday

## 3. Narrative Voice Rules (non-negotiable)
1. Write for the EAR, not the eye: short sentences, natural contractions, rhythm over grammar.
2. Second person early: address "you" within the first 60 seconds.
3. Concrete over abstract: one vivid image beats three adjectives. "A coffee cup left half-full on the desk" > "signs of a sudden departure."
4. One idea per sentence. If a sentence needs a comma to survive, split it.
5. No filler phrases: never "in this episode we will," "without further ado," "let's dive in."
6. Emotional temperature: warm and steady. Peaks of tension are earned through pacing, not exclamation.
7. Explain the mechanism, never issue the command. "Here is why the number stayed in your throat" — not "learn to negotiate."
8. Never diagnose the listener's character. Describe the situation so precisely that they recognise themselves without being told what they are.
9. **Money rule:** never give investment advice, never name a product to buy, never predict a market. Explain the behaviour, and let the listener draw the conclusion. Every specific figure is an illustration, never a recommendation.

## 4. Hook Formula (first 30 seconds)
Pattern: [Specific strange detail] → [why it shouldn't be possible] → [personal stake for the listener] → [promise, no reveal]

Reference-quality examples of the standard:
- "In 1977, an Ohio factory promoted its worst engineer, and output went up eleven percent. The man they passed over had quietly solved every problem on that floor. He had also never once said so out loud."
- "You have rehearsed a number in your head for an entire meeting and never said it. Then someone with half your workload asked for more, and got it. Tonight, what is actually happening in those ninety seconds."
- "A man received four hundred thousand dollars and was broke in eleven months. He did not gamble it, he did not buy a boat, and he can account for almost every dollar. That is the part worth explaining."

Never open with: a greeting, the show name, a book title, a statistic with no story attached, or a question the listener can answer with "no."

## 5. Retention Architecture
- Open loop in the hook → close it only in the final 2 minutes
- Structure is always case → mechanism → tool. Open with one specific person in one specific situation. Never open with the principle.
- Behavioural material enters only as a working procedure at the moment it is needed — what to actually do with the thought, in this situation. Never as a study citation, never as a name-drop, never as a summary of someone's book.
- New micro-question or turn every 60–90 seconds
- Pattern interrupt every ~3 minutes: a short punchy line (under 6 words), a direct address, or a "but here's where it gets strange"
- Mid-episode re-hook at ~40% mark: remind the listener of the unanswered question in fresh words
- **Second case at ~60%:** a different person, same mechanism, opposite outcome. This is what earns the 18–24 minute length instead of padding it.
- Ending: resolve the loop, then ONE final lingering thought (a question or image) — no summary recap, no "thanks for listening" padding

## 6. Language Style
- Reading level: grade 6–7 vocabulary, adult ideas
- Sentence length: average 8–12 words, deliberate variation
- Banned words: unleash, delve, dive, journey, game-changer, revolutionary, elevate, tapestry, mindset, hustle, grind, alpha, sigma, unlock, empower, level up, crush it, secret weapon, passive income, financial freedom, wealth mindset, money blocks, abundance
- Numbers: always specific ("$4,200", "eleven minutes") never vague ("a lot of money", "a short time")
- Currency: use dollars in the script. Never mix currencies inside one example.
- Every claim about behaviour attaches to a situation, not to a personality type. Say what happened, not what kind of person does it.

## 7. Quality Bar (self-check before output)
- Could the listener retell the hook to a friend in one sentence? If not, rewrite it.
- Is there any 60-second stretch with no new information or tension? Cut or compress it.
- Does every section earn its place, or is it there to hit a word count?
- Would this episode still stand if every researcher's name were deleted? If not, the research is decorating instead of working.
- Is there a single sentence a viewer could screenshot as financial advice? If yes, rewrite it as a description of what someone did.

## 8. Authenticity Gate (YouTube inauthentic-content policy)
Fail any one of these and the episode does not ship.
- **Named point of view.** The narrator is a consistent character with opinions, not a neutral reader. At least one moment per episode where the narrator takes a position the listener could disagree with.
- **Original framing.** The episode is not a restatement of a book, an article, or another video. The case, the ordering, and the conclusion are assembled here.
- **Format variation.** Rotate across episodes: single-case deep dive / two-case comparison / reconstructed timeline / a question examined from three angles. Never four consecutive episodes on the same template.
- **No verbatim reading.** Nothing in the script is a paraphrase-free lift from a source.
- **Visual variation.** The storyboard does not reuse the same shot rhythm as the previous episode.
